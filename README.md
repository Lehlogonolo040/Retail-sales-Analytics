***** OVERVIEW OF THE CASE STUDY *****

The Sales Case Study analyzes 1,053 days of retail sales data to evaluate sales performance, pricing, profitability, and customer demand. The project focuses on daily selling price, gross profit performance, promotional pricing, and price elasticity to identify business trends and opportunities for improving revenue and profitability.

***** DATA ANALYSIS AND INSIGHTS *****

. Data Processing and Preparation
- Raw sales data was processed and prepared for analysis.
- Daily sales, cost of sales, and quantity sold were used to calculate key performance metrics.
- Calculated daily Price per Unit, Gross Profit %, Gross Profit per Unit, and Gross Profit per Unit %.
- Created monthly, annual, and day-of-week summaries to identify performance patterns.

. Sales and Profitability Analysis
- Total sales reached R186.91 million across the analysis period.
- More than 5.2 million units were sold.
- Total Cost of Sales of R194.03 million exceeded total sales, resulting in an overall gross loss of R7.12 million (-3.81%).
- Profitability varied throughout the period, with performance showing improvement during 2016.

. Pricing Analysis
- The average daily unit price was R37.07, compared with a volume-weighted average of R35.40.
- The lower volume-weighted average indicates that higher sales volumes were generally achieved at lower selling prices.
- Promotional periods showed clear reductions in unit selling prices.

. Price Elasticity Analysis
- Three promotional periods were identified and evaluated using Price Elasticity of Demand.
- All three promotional periods showed highly elastic demand, with elasticity values ranging from -5.56 to -34.35.
- Promotion 2 produced the strongest response, with a 14.7% price reduction resulting in more than a 500% increase in units sold.
- The findings indicate that promotional pricing can significantly increase customer demand, although profitability must also be considered.

. Additional Insights
- Sales followed a seasonal pattern, with notable peaks during March 2014, August 2014, and May 2015.
- Monthly sales volumes declined from mid-2016 onward.
- Fridays and Saturdays recorded approximately 70–90% higher average daily sales than midweek trading days.
- These patterns provide opportunities for improved inventory planning, staffing, and promotional timing.

***** RECOMMENDATIONS *****

- Continue using targeted promotional pricing where increased demand justifies the price reduction.
- Monitor gross profit closely to ensure higher sales volumes translate into improved profitability.
- Review pricing and supplier costs to address the overall negative gross profit margin.
- Align staffing and inventory levels with high-performing trading days, particularly Fridays and Saturdays.
- Use price elasticity analysis to guide future promotional and pricing decisions.

***** SUMMARY AND INSIGHTS *****

- Built a data-driven sales analysis framework to evaluate daily, monthly, annual, and day-of-week performance.
- Calculated key sales, pricing, and profitability metrics from daily sales data.
- Identified highly elastic customer demand during promotional periods.
- Identified seasonal sales patterns and high-performing trading days.
- Developed an interactive dashboard to communicate key business performance indicators and trends.
- Generated actionable recommendations focused on pricing, promotions, profitability, and operational planning.
